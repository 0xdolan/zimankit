# 14. The past tense II: transitive verbs and ergativity

**Level:** 🟡 Intermediate · lesson 14 of 23

**On this page:** [The surprise](#the-surprise) · [What is going on](#what-is-going-on) · [Where the clitic goes](#where-the-clitic-goes) · [Generated paradigms](#generated-paradigms) · [In sentences](#in-sentences) · [Why it matters](#why-it-matters) · [Practice](#practice) · [Summary](#summary)

## The surprise

Compare:

| | present | past |
|---|---|---|
| intransitive *come* | دێ**م** *I come* | هات**م** *I came* |
| transitive *do* | دەکە**م** *I do* | کرد**م** *I did* |

So far nothing looks different. But try *I ate bread*:

<!-- gen:sentences ipa=1
من نان دەخۆم. | I eat bread.
من نانم خوارد. | I ate bread.
-->
| # | Kurdish | Latin | IPA | English |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | من نان دەخۆم. | min nan dexom. | /m(ɯ)n naːn dæxoːm/ | I eat bread. |
| 2 | من نانم خوارد. | min nanim xward. | /m(ɯ)n naːn(ɯ)m xwaːɾd/ | I ate bread. |
<!-- /gen -->

In the past, the **-م** that means *I* has jumped from the verb onto **نان**! This is **ergativity**, the most famous feature of Kurdish grammar.

## What is going on

In the past tenses of **transitive** verbs, the doer (the *agent*) is not marked by a verb ending. It is marked by a **personal clitic**, the same م ت ی مان تان یان you learned as *my, your, …* in lesson 11. Think of *I ate bread* as something like *bread was eaten by-me*.

| person | clitic | خواردن *to eat*, no object | with the object نان |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1sg | -م | خواردم *I ate* | نانم خوارد |
| 2sg | -ت | خواردت *you ate* | نانت خوارد |
| 3sg | -ی | خواردی *he/she ate* | نانی خوارد |
| 1pl | -مان | خواردمان *we ate* | نانمان خوارد |
| 2pl | -تان | خواردتان *you ate* | نانتان خوارد |
| 3pl | -یان | خواردیان *they ate* | نانیان خوارد |

## Where the clitic goes

The agent clitic attaches to the **first word of the verb phrase**, the first thing that comes before the verb itself:

1. if there is an **object**, to the object: **نانم** خوارد;
2. if the verb is **negative**, to نە: **نەم**خوارد *I didn't eat*;
3. otherwise to the verb: **خواردم**.

The pronoun subject (من، تۆ…) can be added for emphasis, but it is not what carries the person: **من** نانم خوارد.

## In sentences

<!-- gen:sentences ipa=1
نامەیەکم نووسی. | I wrote a letter.
کتێبەکەت خوێند؟ | Did you read the book?
ئەو هیچی نەگوت. | He didn't say anything.
ئێمە ئۆتۆمبێلێکمان کڕی. | We bought a car.
پیاوەکەیان بینی. | They saw the man.
-->
| # | Kurdish | Latin | IPA | English |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | نامەیەکم نووسی. | nameyekim nûsî. | /naːmæjæk(ɯ)m nuːsiː/ | I wrote a letter. |
| 2 | کتێبەکەت خوێند؟ | kitêbeket xwênd? | /k(ɯ)teːbækæt xweːnd/ | Did you read the book? |
| 3 | ئەو هیچی نەگوت. | ew hîçî negut. | /ʔæw hiːtʃiː næɡut/ | He didn't say anything. |
| 4 | ئێمە ئۆتۆمبێلێکمان کڕی. | ême otombêlêkman kirrî. | /ʔeːmæ ʔoːtoːmbeːleːkmaːn k(ɯ)riː/ | We bought a car. |
| 5 | پیاوەکەیان بینی. | pyawekeyan bînî. | /pjaːwækæjaːn biːniː/ | They saw the man. |
<!-- /gen -->

Notice **پیاوەکەیان بینی**: -یان on پیاوەکە here means *they*, not *their*. Only the past transitive verb tells you which. In the present, پیاوەکەیان دەبینن would mean *they see their man*.

## Why it matters

Ergativity is where learners and grammar checkers make the most mistakes. Three typical errors:

| wrong | right | problem |
|---|---|---|
| \* من نان خواردم | من نانم خوارد | the clitic must go on the first element (the object) |
| \* نانم خواردم | نانم خوارد | the agent is marked twice |
| \* نەخواردم | نەمخوارد | in the negative the clitic goes on نە |

The first one, *من نان خواردم*, is common in speech in some dialects. The written standard prefers نانم خوارد.

## Practice

1. *You wrote.* — *You wrote the letter* (نامەکە).
2. *They didn't buy bread.*
3. *We saw.* — *We didn't see.*

<details><summary>Answers</summary>

1. نووسیت — نامەکەت نووسی
2. نانیان نەکڕی
3. بینیمان — نەمانبینی

</details>

## Summary

* Past of **transitive** verbs: the agent is a **clitic** (م ت ی مان تان یان), not an ending.
* The clitic sits on the first element before the verb: object, نە, or the verb itself.
* Intransitive verbs keep normal endings (هاتم); only transitive verbs are ergative.
