# 4. Consonants and pronunciation

**Level:** 🟢 Beginner · lesson 4 of 23

**On this page:** [Consonants you already know](#consonants-you-already-know) · [Sounds English does not have](#sounds-english-does-not-have) · [Pairs that change meaning](#pairs-that-change-meaning) · [Stress](#stress) · [Practice](#practice) · [Summary](#summary)

## Consonants you already know

Most Sorani consonants sound much as in English: ب b, پ p, ت t, د d, ف f, ک k, گ g (always hard, as in *go*), م m, ن n, س s, ز z, ه h (always pronounced, also at the end of a word), و w, ی y.

A few English sounds have their own letter:

| letter | IPA | English sound | example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **ش** | ʃ | *sh* in *ship* | شار *city* |
| **چ** | tʃ | *ch* in *church* | چاو *eye* |
| **ج** | dʒ | *j* in *jam* | جوان *beautiful* |
| **ژ** | ʒ | *s* in *measure* | ژن *woman* |

## Sounds English does not have

| letter | IPA | how to make it | example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **خ** | x | the *ch* of Scottish *loch* or German *Bach*: friction at the back of the mouth | خەو *sleep* |
| **غ** | ɣ | the voiced partner of خ, a gargled *g*, close to the French *r* | غەریب *stranger* |
| **ق** | q | a *k* made further back, against the soft palate's very end | قەڵەم *pen* |
| **ح** | ħ | a strong breathy *h* from the throat | حەوت *seven* |
| **ع** | ʕ | a tightening of the throat, voiced | عەقڵ *mind* |
| **ڕ** | r | a rolled *r* (Spanish *perro*) | ڕۆژ *day* |
| **ڵ** | ɫ | a “dark” *l*, tongue pulled back (English *full*) | ماڵ *house* |

ح and ع come mainly in words borrowed from Arabic. Many speakers soften them to ه and ئ, but the spelling keeps them.

## Pairs that change meaning

Some words differ only in one of these sounds, so the difference matters:

| pair | meaning |
|---|---|
| کەر — کەڕ | donkey — deaf |
| کار — کاڵ | work — unripe, pale |

**ر vs ڕ.** ر is a single tap of the tongue (like the *tt* in American *butter*); ڕ is a trill. At the very start of a word only the trill occurs, so a word-initial *r* is always written ڕ: ڕۆژ, ڕێگا *road*.

**ل vs ڵ.** ڵ never starts a word. Compare گوڵ *flower* with the clear *l* of لاو *young man*.

## Stress

Sorani words are normally stressed on the **last syllable**: *ki-TÊB* “book”, *zi-MAN* “language”. Endings you will learn later (the definite *-eke*, the plural *-an*) take the stress with them: *ki-tê-be-KE* “the book”. Verbs are different: the prefix دە-, نا- or ب- usually carries the stress (*DE-çim* “I go”).

## Practice

1. Which word starts with a trilled *r*: **ڕۆژ** or **بەرد**?
2. Say خەو *xew* and then the same word with ه instead of خ. Feel where the friction is.
3. Find the dark *l* in: گوڵ، لاو، ماڵ.

<details><summary>Answers</summary>

1. ڕۆژ: the trill is written ڕ, and every word-initial r is a trill. The ر in بەرد is a tap.
2. خ is made at the back of the mouth (velar); ه is just breath.
3. گوڵ and ماڵ (ڵ); لاو has a clear l.

</details>

## Summary

* Learn خ غ ق ح ع ڕ ڵ, the sounds English lacks.
* A word-initial *r* is always the trill ڕ; ڵ never starts a word.
* Stress normally falls on the last syllable; verb prefixes take the stress.
