# 7. Nouns: gender, a / the, and plurals

**Level:** 🟢 Beginner · lesson 7 of 23

**On this page:** [Masculine and feminine](#masculine-and-feminine) · [“The”: nothing](#the-nothing) · [“A”: -ek](#a--ek) · [Plural: often nothing!](#plural-often-nothing) · [The direct forms](#the-direct-forms) · [Practice](#practice) · [Summary](#summary)

## Masculine and feminine

Every Kurmanji noun is **masculine (m)** or **feminine (f)**. For people it follows sex (*bav* father m, *jin* woman f); for everything else you must learn it with the word, as in German or French. The gender does not change the noun itself, but it chooses the endings of lessons 8 and 9, so **always learn nouns with their gender**:

| Kurdish | Arabic script | IPA | syllables | meaning |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **bav** | باڤ | /baːv/ | baːv | father |
| **bira** | برا | /bɪɾaː/ | bɪ.ɾaː | brother |
| **mirov** | مرۆڤ | /mɪɾoːv/ | mɪ.ɾoːv | person, human, man |
| **jin** | ژن | /ʒɪn/ | ʒɪn | woman; wife |
| **keç** | کەچ | /kɛtʃ/ | kɛtʃ | girl; daughter |
| **mal** | مال | /maːl/ | maːl | house, home |
| **bajar** | باژار | /baːʒaːɾ/ | baː.ʒaːɾ | city |
| **pirtûk** | پرتووک | /pɪɾtuːk/ | pɪɾ.tuːk | book |
| **ziman** | زمان | /zɪmaːn/ | zɪ.maːn | language; tongue |

## “The”: nothing

A bare noun is **definite** or generic: *jin* “the woman” or “woman”. There is no word for *the*.

## “A”: -ek

Add **-ek** (after a vowel **-yek**): *jinek* “a woman”, *çiyayek* “a mountain”, *xaniyek* “a house”. For *some* (plural) use **hin**: *hin pirtûk* “some books”.

## Plural: often nothing!

In the **direct** case the plural is **unmarked**: *keç* = “the girl” or “the girls”. The verb and any endings show the number:

<!-- gen:sentences ipa=1
Keç hat. | The girl came.
Keç hatin. | The girls came.
Jinek hat. | A woman came.
Hin mirov hatin. | Some people came.
-->
| # | Kurdish | Arabic script | IPA | English |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Keç hat. | کەچ هات. | /kɛtʃ haːt/ | The girl came. |
| 2 | Keç hatin. | کەچ هاتن. | /kɛtʃ haːtɯn/ | The girls came. |
| 3 | Jinek hat. | ژنەک هات. | /ʒɪnɛk haːt/ | A woman came. |
| 4 | Hin mirov hatin. | هن مرۆڤ هاتن. | /hɪn mɪɾoːv haːtɯn/ | Some people came. |
<!-- /gen -->

In the oblique case (lesson 9) and with ezafe (lesson 8) the plural **is** marked: *-an*, *-ên*.

## Practice

1. *a book*, *a mountain*, *a house* (xanî).
2. What is the gender of *mal*, *bajar*, *jin*?

<details><summary>Answers</summary>

1. pirtûkek, çiyayek, xaniyek.
2. *mal* f, *bajar* m, *jin* f.

</details>

## Summary

* Every noun is m or f: learn the gender with the noun.
* *the* = bare noun; *a* = **-ek / -yek**; *some* = **hin**.
* The direct plural is unmarked; the verb shows the number.
