# 2. The Hawar alphabet

**Level:** 🟢 Beginner · lesson 2 of 23

**On this page:** [The 31 letters](#the-31-letters) · [Letters that surprise English speakers](#letters-that-surprise-english-speakers) · [Upper and lower case](#upper-and-lower-case) · [Extra letters some writers use](#extra-letters-some-writers-use) · [Practice](#practice) · [Summary](#summary)

## The 31 letters

**A B C Ç D E Ê F G H I Î J K L M N O P Q R S Ş T U Û V W X Y Z**

Twenty-six are the letters of English, but several are pronounced differently, and five have marks: **ç ê î ş û**. The marks are part of the letter. *e* and *ê*, *i* and *î*, *u* and *û* are different vowels, and *s / ş* and *c / ç* are different consonants.

## Letters that surprise English speakers

| letter | sound | not like English | example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **c** | *j* in *jam* | not *k* or *s* | ciwan *young* |
| **ç** | *ch* in *church* | | çav *eye* |
| **j** | *s* in *measure* | not *j* in *jam* | jin *woman* |
| **ş** | *sh* in *ship* | | şev *night* |
| **x** | *ch* in Scottish *loch* | not *ks* | xew *sleep* |
| **q** | a *k* far back in the throat | | qelem *pen* |
| **y** | *y* in *yes* | always a consonant | yek *one* |
| **w** | *w* in *wet* | | welat *country* |
| **h** | always pronounced | also at the end | hesp *horse* |

## Upper and lower case

Capitals are used as in English: at the start of a sentence and for names (*Amed, Rojda, Kurdistan*). Two capitals need care on computers:

* **I** is the capital of **i**, and **Î** the capital of **î**. Never use the Turkish dotted **İ** or dotless **ı**: they are different letters and break spelling and search. zimankit's `lower()` and `upper()` handle this correctly (lesson 23).

## Extra letters some writers use

For sounds in Arabic loanwords, some writers add **ḧ** (a strong *h*), **ẍ** (a gargled *g*) and an apostrophe for the throat sound *ʕ*: *'erd* “earth”. The standard alphabet writes them as *h*, *x* and nothing (*erd*). zimankit understands both.

## Practice

1. How do you pronounce **c** in *ciwan*, and **j** in *jin*?
2. Which of these letters are vowels: a, ç, ê, î, ş, û?
3. Read aloud: *bav, çav, jin, şev, xew*.

<details><summary>Answers</summary>

1. *c* = *j* in *jam* (/dʒ/); *j* = *s* in *measure* (/ʒ/).
2. a, ê, î, û. (ç and ş are consonants.)
3. /baːv/ father, /tʃaːv/ eye, /ʒɪn/ woman, /ʃɛv/ night, /xɛw/ sleep.

</details>

## Summary

* 31 letters: the 26 letters of English plus ç ê î ş û.
* c = /dʒ/, ç = /tʃ/, j = /ʒ/, ş = /ʃ/, x = /x/.
* The marks matter: e ≠ ê, i ≠ î, u ≠ û, s ≠ ş, c ≠ ç.
